ChartDesk · Demo guide

Try the ChartDesk demo, step by step

The demo is a working copy of ChartDesk for a fictional long-term care home with two units, 56 residents and 377 scanned, faxed and handwritten pages. Everything you see is fictional. It takes about ten minutes to try every part.

Before you start

  • Every resident and record is fictional. Names, faces and pages were generated for testing. Never add a page about a real person.
  • The demo is shared. Other visitors may be trying it at the same time. Reset demo data, at the bottom of the menu, puts everything back to the start.
  • The demo date is fixed at November 22, 2026, so dates on the pages and the due list always agree.
  • Use any device. On a phone, the menu is behind the button at the top left.

A ten-minute tour

  1. Fax and scan inbox

    Every new page waits here. ChartDesk shows the page image, what it read, the resident it suggests and any follow-up it found. Nothing is filed until a nurse confirms.

    Try it: Open the Consult note from the anticoagulation clinic: the name on the page is misspelled, and ChartDesk flags it as a close spelling. Then try a Handwritten note, and press Confirm & file. To add your own page, use Choose photo or scan with a fictional page only.

  2. A resident's chart

    Each resident has one chart organized by importance: goals of care first, then lab trends, documents, follow-ups, medications, and the family doctor and pharmacy from before admission.

    Try it: Go to Residents and open Margaret Ellis. Each lab value has a source link.

  3. The original page, beside what was read

    Every document can be opened beside the text ChartDesk read from it, so anyone can check it. Read again, live sends the image to the page reader in front of you.

    Try it: In Margaret Ellis's chart, click any document under Documents, then Read again, live.

  4. Find: ask in plain words

    Search every filed page by exact words or in plain language. The answer cites the pages it used; each citation opens the original with the words highlighted.

    Try it: Click a suggested question, or ask Who had a high INR? Then ask something the record does not contain: ChartDesk answers Not found in the record instead of guessing.

  5. Doctor day

    A pack for the physician's weekly round: for each resident, this week's nursing notes, results that are newly out of range or getting worse, new outside reports and what is due, each with its source. Residents with nothing new are listed separately.

    Try it: Mark a resident Seen, no change, filter by unit, or press Print pack.

  6. Referrals and transfers

    The nurse writes the reason in their own words; ChartDesk gathers goals of care, history, medications and allergies, the latest results and the pre-admission family doctor and pharmacy, each with its source.

    Try it: Press Build package with the example filled in. Try a vague reason such as “knee pain”: it is refused. Approve & save adds a follow-up to confirm the referral arrived.

  7. Change in condition

    When a resident's condition changes, ChartDesk drafts an SBAR for the physician from the nurse's own words and the chart, with goals of care shown first. It does not diagnose or suggest orders.

    Try it: Press Draft SBAR, edit any section, then Approve & save to chart. The saved note appears in the resident's documents.

  8. Behaviour trends

    Seven days of handwritten behaviour observation sheets become an hour-by-hour grid, with simple counts of when responsive behaviours happen.

    Try it: Choose different residents from the list to compare patterns.

What the demo does not do yet

  • Pages are added by upload or come preloaded. In a home, the fax line and scanner would feed the inbox directly.
  • It is not connected to an electronic health record or pharmacy system.
  • The demo has one shared login. A real deployment has a named account and role for each person.
