Long-term care · ChartDesk · In development

ChartDesk: the paper side of the chart, finally searchable

In many Ontario long-term care homes, much of a resident's record still lives on paper. Lab results and hospital reports arrive by fax and go into binders; behaviour sheets are filled in by hand. ChartDesk is a scan-to-chart desk for the nurse in charge, built with the nurses and physicians who do this work every day.

What we heard on the floor

We spent time on site in an Ontario long-term care home with nurses, office staff and the attending physician. The message was consistent: the information exists, but it is hard to find when it matters. As one team member put it, "We're wasting so much time to look for things." When a resident's condition changes, the nurse calling the physician does not always have the latest results and goals of care in hand.

What ChartDesk does

  • Capture & file. Faxes and scans are read and matched to the right resident. A nurse confirms with one tap; nothing is filed on its own.
  • Find. Search by exact words or in plain language. Every answer links to the original page, and if something is not in the record, ChartDesk says so instead of guessing.
  • Due & overdue. Follow-ups mentioned in documents, such as a repeat blood test, become a visible due list for each resident.
  • Change in condition. Drafts an SBAR for the on-call physician with goals of care shown first, to help avoid unnecessary hospital transfers.
  • Behaviour trends. Handwritten behaviour observation sheets become trends the care team can actually see.

People stay in charge

ChartDesk organizes, finds and drafts. It does not make clinical decisions. Every draft is reviewed by a nurse or physician before it reaches the chart, in line with our clinician-in-the-loop principle.

Privacy by design

  • The home remains the custodian of resident information; we act only as its agent under a written agreement.
  • No resident data is used until a privacy agreement and privacy impact assessment are in place.
  • Data is stored in Canada, encrypted and access-logged, and is never used to train AI models.
  • Research and development use realistic test records, not real resident data.

Where we are

ChartDesk is in research and prototyping, shaped by on-site discovery and a long-term care physician as our clinical lead. We are looking for Ontario homes interested in helping test it. The same discipline behind our workflow automation and neuroanalytics work applies here: measured, reviewable and private.

Related terms

  • Clinician in the loopA design principle in which AI assists, drafts and flags, but a qualified clinician reviews and makes every clinical decision.
  • Care coordinationOrganizing a patient's care across providers, programs and sites so that assessments, follow-ups and handoffs happen on time.
  • SBARSituation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation: a standard structure nurses use to tell a physician about a change in a patient's condition.
  • Goals of careA resident's documented wishes and agreed plan for the kind of care they want, including whether a hospital transfer fits those wishes.
