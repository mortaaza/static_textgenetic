ChartDesk: the paper side of the chart, finally searchable

In many long-term care homes, much of a resident's record still lives on paper. Lab results and hospital reports arrive by fax and go into binders; behaviour sheets and notes are written by hand. ChartDesk reads those pages, files them to the right resident, and puts the right information, with its source, in front of the nurse and physician. A working prototype is online today.

Who it is for

Older adults living in long-term care, many of them living with dementia, and the people who care for them: the nurse in charge, the attending physician or nurse practitioner, and the office staff who handle referrals and transfers. Every resident deserves care informed by their whole story, including the parts that are still on paper.

What we observed

In October 2026 we spent three days on site with a long-term care team in Ontario and the office of its attending physician. We sat with the nursing and administration desk, the reception team, the physician during his weekly round workflow, and the clinic staff who manage referrals. What we observed:

  • Finding information takes much of the day. As one team member put it, "We're wasting so much time to look for things." Faxed lab results are filed on paper in the chart, not scanned.
  • Results are retyped. When a result needs action, staff search the chart for the latest related values and type them into a message for the physician.
  • Doctor days run on paper. A week of concerns and handwritten behaviour sheets are gathered for the physician to review, sign and file.
  • Referrals and transfers are assembled by hand, with a different form for each destination: 5 to 10 minutes each, if nobody interrupts.
  • Follow-ups are hard to track, because they sit inside progress notes and outside reports.
  • The same information is entered several times across separate systems and paper.

When information is fragmented, care coordination becomes harder, and the risk of missed information and delayed care goes up.

What we built

A working web application, built in November 2026 directly from those observations, and tested on realistic records we created for the purpose.

Measured on our own test pages, degraded to look faxed and photocopied. Real-world accuracy will be measured with partner homes.

  • Capture and file. Faxes, scans and phone photos are read and matched to the right resident by name and date of birth. A nurse confirms with one tap; nothing is filed on its own.
  • Two views of every record. An exact copy of every original page, and an electronic chart organized by importance: goals of care first, then results, follow-ups and documents.
  • Find. Ask in plain words. The answer cites the exact page it came from; if the record does not say, ChartDesk answers "Not found in the record" instead of guessing.
  • Due and overdue. Follow-ups written inside reports, such as "repeat potassium in 48 to 72 hours", become a visible due list.
  • Doctor-day pack. Before the physician's round: what changed this week for each resident, with sources, and who has nothing new.
  • Referrals and transfers. The nurse writes the reason; ChartDesk adds goals of care, history, medications, allergies and recent results, then tracks whether the referral was received.
  • Change in condition. Drafts an SBAR for the physician with goals of care shown first, to help avoid unnecessary hospital transfers.
  • Behaviour trends. Handwritten behaviour observation sheets become an hour-by-hour pattern the team can see.
  • Activity log. Every sign-in, search, chart view, filing and approval is recorded.

How it works

  1. Read. A vision model reads each page image, including faxes, photocopies and handwriting, and keeps the text exactly as written.
  2. Match. Name and date of birth are compared with the resident list. A one-letter misspelling is flagged for checking; a page for someone who does not live in the home is not matched.
  3. Extract. Lab values and follow-up instructions are pulled into the chart, each linked to its line on the original page.
  4. Find and draft. Search ranks the matching pages; the assistant answers only from those pages and cites them. Drafts (SBAR, referral, round pack) use only facts from the chart.
  5. Approve. A nurse or physician reviews every filing and draft before it reaches the chart. ChartDesk does not diagnose or suggest treatment.

People stay in charge

ChartDesk organizes, finds and drafts. It does not make clinical decisions. Every draft is reviewed by a nurse or physician before it reaches the chart, in line with our clinician-in-the-loop principle.

Privacy by design

  • Designed for Ontario's PHIPA and Canada's PIPEDA. The home remains the custodian of resident information; we act only as its agent under a written agreement.
  • No resident data is used until a privacy agreement and privacy impact assessment are in place.
  • Before any resident information is used, it will be stored and processed in Canada, encrypted and access-logged. It is never used to train AI models.
  • Research and development use realistic test records, not real resident data. The demo contains fictional people only.

Related terms
