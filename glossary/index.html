Glossary

Brain-health AI and applied-AI glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms we use in neuroanalytics, clinical workflow automation and predictive AI — written for clinicians, public-sector buyers and partners, not just engineers.

  • Care coordinationOrganizing a patient's care across providers, programs and sites so that assessments, follow-ups and handoffs happen on time.
  • Clinician in the loopA design principle in which AI assists, drafts and flags, but a qualified clinician reviews and makes every clinical decision.
  • De-identificationRemoving or transforming personal identifiers in health data so that individuals cannot reasonably be identified, enabling research use.
  • Digital biomarkerAn objective, measurable feature derived from digital data — sensors, devices, software — that reflects a biological or clinical state.
  • Digital phenotypingCharacterizing a person's health and behaviour from data generated by everyday digital devices, such as phones and wearables.
  • Federated learningA way of training a machine-learning model across several organizations without moving their raw data to a central place.
  • Freezing of gaitA sudden, brief inability to start or continue walking despite the intention to move, common in advanced Parkinson's disease.
  • Innovative Solutions Canada — Testing StreamA Government of Canada program that procures late-stage Canadian innovations (TRL 7–9) and tests them in real federal operational environments.
  • Longitudinal dataRepeated measurements of the same individuals over time, as opposed to a single snapshot.
  • Mild cognitive impairment (MCI)A measurable decline in memory or thinking that is greater than expected for age but not severe enough to be dementia.
  • NeuroanalyticsThe use of data analysis and machine learning on clinical, behavioural and sensor signals to measure and predict brain health over time.
  • Parkinson's disease subtypesGroups of people with Parkinson's disease who share a pattern of symptoms, progression rate or underlying biology, used to tailor care and research.
  • Predictive maintenanceUsing data and machine learning to forecast when equipment is likely to fail, so maintenance happens before the failure instead of after it or on a fixed schedule.
  • Reassessment trackingAutomatically computing and monitoring when each patient is due for a scheduled clinical reassessment, and who is responsible.
  • Technology Readiness Level (TRL)A 1-to-9 scale that describes how mature a technology is, from basic principles (TRL 1) to a system proven in real operations (TRL 9).
