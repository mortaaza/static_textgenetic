Glossary

Digital biomarker

An objective, measurable feature derived from digital data — sensors, devices, software — that reflects a biological or clinical state.

A biomarker is any measurable indicator of a biological state: blood pressure, a lab value, a score on a scale. A digital biomarker is one computed from data collected through digital means — wearable sensors, a phone's microphone, a gait mat, software that logs how a task is performed. Examples in neurology include stride-length variability from a gait sensor, speech pacing from recorded voice, or reaction-time distributions from a cognitive test on a tablet.

Digital biomarkers are attractive because they can be captured frequently, cheaply and outside the clinic, which makes them well suited to tracking slow change. They are only useful once validated: a candidate feature must be shown to be reliable, to correlate with a clinically meaningful outcome, and to change in a way that matters. Most of the work is in the capture and the validation, not in the model.
