Glossary

Longitudinal data

Repeated measurements of the same individuals over time, as opposed to a single snapshot.

Longitudinal data records the same variables for the same people at multiple points — assessment scores every six months, gait measures every visit, medication changes as they happen. It is the only way to observe change directly: cross-sectional data (one measurement per person) can show that older patients score lower, but not whether a given patient is declining.

Longitudinal data is harder to collect well. Measurements must be consistent across visits and sites, timing must be recorded accurately, and missing visits must be handled honestly. In practice, the quality of a neuroanalytics program is decided by the quality of its longitudinal capture.

Why it matters to our work

Our workflow layer exists partly to make longitudinal capture routine: when reassessments are tracked automatically, the data needed for progression analytics accumulates as a by-product of good care.

Related terms

  • NeuroanalyticsThe use of data analysis and machine learning on clinical, behavioural and sensor signals to measure and predict brain health over time.
  • De-identificationRemoving or transforming personal identifiers in health data so that individuals cannot reasonably be identified, enabling research use.
  • Reassessment trackingAutomatically computing and monitoring when each patient is due for a scheduled clinical reassessment, and who is responsible.
